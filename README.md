## Overview

**simeq** (Simulation for Equity) is an individual-level, stochastic, open-cohort microsimulation model for projecting BMI trajectories, health burden and healthcare costs by sex and education. Its Portuguese application, **SIMEQ-PT**, covers adults aged 20–84 from 1998 to 2040.

The model comprises three modules:

| Module | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **I — BMI trajectories** | Simulates annual BMI change with percentile-specific transition models fitted to synthetic cohorts from the Portuguese National Health Survey (1998, 2005, 2014, 2019), combined with ageing, education upgrading, mortality and new entrants |
| **II — Health outcomes** | Estimates quality-adjusted life years (QALYs), obesity-attributable QALY loss and years of life lost (YLL); functions for years lived with disability (YLD) and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) are also included but are not used in SIMEQ-PT |
| **III — Economic costs** | Projects direct healthcare costs attributable to high BMI and obesity |

## Data

The full analysis uses the **Portuguese National Health Survey** (Inquérito Nacional de Saúde), whose individual-level data are not publicly available. Access can be requested from [INE (Statistics Portugal)](https://www.ine.pt/) and [INSA](https://www.insa.min-saude.pt/). No survey records are distributed with this repository.

Reference data bundled in `inst/extdata/`:

- **GBD 2019 BMI relative risks** (`gbd_rr_2019.xlsx`), from the [Global Burden of Disease study](https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results/)
- **Portuguese life table** (`prob_dying_qx.xlsx`), age- and sex-specific probabilities of death (per 1,000)
- **Demographic validation data** (`demografia_ine_98_2025.xls`, `eurostat_clean.xlsx`), from INE and Eurostat

Two fully synthetic datasets ship with the package; no survey record is used in either:

- `simeq_synthetic_panel`: four synthetic survey waves (1998, 2005, 2014, 2019; 5,000 adults each) with the same structure as the National Health Survey panel, generated by `data-raw/create_synthetic_panel.R`. It runs all three modules end to end; see the vignette below.
- `simeq_synthetic_pop`: a small synthetic population (n = 2,000, `data-raw/create_synthetic_population.R`) for examples and tests.

Results from the synthetic data are illustrative only and are not SIMEQ-PT estimates.

## Quick start

```r
library(simeq)
library(dplyr)

# Life table: long format (age, sex, qx), ages 20-84
life_table <- readxl::read_excel(system.file("extdata", "prob_dying_qx.xlsx", package = "simeq")) |>
  transmute(age = as.integer(age), men = as.numeric(qx_m) / 1000, women = as.numeric(qx_w) / 1000) |>
  tidyr::pivot_longer(c(men, women), names_to = "sex", values_to = "qx") |>
  filter(age >= 20, age <= 84) |>
  simeq_validate_life_table()

# BMI relative risks of death (GBD 2019)
rr_bmi <- read_gbd_bmi_rr(path = system.file("extdata", "gbd_rr_2019.xlsx", package = "simeq")) |>
  transform_bmi_relative_risks()

# Settings and a synthetic baseline population
settings <- simeq_settings(initial_year = 2019, projection_end_year = 2030, n_simulations = 10, seed = 4321L)
population <- simeq_synthetic_pop
```

A full run also needs a fitted BMI transition model (`simeq_fit_bmi_transition_percentile()`), entrant targets and donor pools; `simeq_run_simulation()` then projects the population.

The vignette *Running simeq end to end with synthetic data* fits the BMI transitions on `simeq_synthetic_panel`, runs the simulation and estimates QALYs, years of life lost and costs (Modules I–III) in under a minute:

## Reproducing SIMEQ-PT (v10)

The SIMEQ-PT results reported in the paper were produced with this version of the package and the files below.

| File | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| `data-raw/prepare_model_inputs.R` | Builds the model inputs from the National Health Survey waves, with self-reported height and weight corrected by the calibration equations (revision 1) |
| `data-raw/clean_2025.qmd`, `data-raw/prepare_nhs_2025_validation.R` | Prepare the 2025 survey used for out-of-sample validation |
| `data-raw/prepare_life_tables.R` | Builds the 1998–2024 year-specific life tables from Human Mortality Database-format tables |
| `mortality_lc/project_life_table_lee_carter_v1.R` | Projects the Portuguese life tables to 2040 (Lee-Carter) |
| `analysis/simeq_pt_analysis_v10.qmd` | Runs modules I–III (50 replications, 1998–2040) and produces all tables and figures |
| `data-raw/compare_v9_v10.R`, `data-raw/diagnose_bmi_distribution_v10.R`, `data-raw/test_transition_tails*.R`, `data-raw/paper_figures_v10.R` | Comparison with the previous version, distribution diagnostics, sensitivity tests of the BMI transitions, and paper figures |

Steps:

1. Place the survey files in `data-raw/national_health_surveys/` (not distributed).
2. Run `data-raw/prepare_model_inputs.R` and `data-raw/prepare_nhs_2025_validation.R`.
3. Download the Portuguese period life tables (Human Mortality Database, 1x1, by sex; not redistributed), save them as `data/life_tables/pt_life_tables_1998_2024_men.csv` and `..._women.csv`, run `data-raw/prepare_life_tables.R`, then `mortality_lc/project_life_table_lee_carter_v1.R` (see `mortality_lc/how_to_use_lee_carter_v1.md`).
4. Render `analysis/simeq_pt_analysis_v10.qmd` from the project root (`quarto render analysis/simeq_pt_analysis_v10.qmd`). Simulation results are written to `results/`.

The analysis also uses the R packages broom, data.table, devtools, flextable, gt, here, knitr, officer, patchwork, purrr, readr, scales and stringr, and Quarto.
